Submission Title (ALL CAPS, must match the PDF exactly)
WHOSE LABEL? ACTOR INTENT VERSUS LISTENER PERCEPTION IN CREMA-D SPEECH EMOTION RECOGNITION

Paper Abstract (146 words; the form allows 200)
CREMA-D results are almost always scored against the filename emotion, which records an actor's instruction rather than what a listener heard. The corpus ships the data needed to check that (68,568 voice-only judgements), and it is seldom used. Listeners recover the intended category for 45.6% of clips; sad reaches 18.2% against a 16.7% floor, because 71.8% of sad-intended clips are heard as neutral. We train eight systems and score identical predictions against both label sets. Swapping the key alone moves a fixed system's accuracy by up to 29.7 points and reorders any table mixing the conventions (tau = 0.58). The intended label is not merely noisier: a classifier recovers acted sadness at 66.6% where listeners reach 18.2%. It is a learnable target with little to do with what anyone hears, and a CREMA-D number's value and its verdict both turn on a choice papers rarely state.
